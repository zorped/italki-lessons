Your sentences

Try to say each one in the past out loud. Then open the natural version.

  • Two weeks ago I wake up and download Instagram.

    Say it naturally

    Two weeks ago, I woke up and downloaded Instagram.

    Two weeks ago is the past, so every verb is past: woke up, downloaded.

  • I see reels maybe two hours and I scared about this time.

    Say it naturally

    I watched reels for about two hours, and I was scared about how much time I had spent.

    Watch videos and reels (see is for noticing). Use for + a length of time. Don't forget was: I was scared.

  • After that I delete up again.

    Say it naturally

    After that, I deleted the app again.

    Delete is a regular verb: deleted. No up after it.

  • I see a video in YouTube where foreign people visit Russian and have so many problem with internet.

    Say it naturally

    I saw a video on YouTube where foreigners visited Russia and had so many problems with the internet.

    Past: saw, visited, had. We say on YouTube. Russia is the country; Russian is the language or the people.

  • When I see them story I knew about new information about this person.

    Say it naturally

    When I see their stories, I learn something new about that person.

    Their goes before a noun (their stories). For a habit, keep both verbs in the present: see … learn.

  • I finished my university and don't go next level like PhD.

    Say it naturally

    I finished university, but I didn't continue to a PhD.

    A finished past event needs didn't, not don't. We say finished university, without my.

Fix it · patterns · quiz

Ask questions in the past

You asked many follow-up questions, and your ideas were clear. Adding did, was or were makes each one sound much more natural.

Pattern 1

Past event → did + base verb

  • Do you visit Moscow two months ago?

    Say it naturally

    Did you visit Moscow two months ago?

    Two months ago is the past. Did + you + base verb.

  • What kind of places you visit in Moscow?

    Say it naturally

    What kind of places did you visit in Moscow?

    After What kind of places, you still need did before you.

  • Where you live? (about a hotel on a trip)

    Say it naturally

    Where did you stay?

    Live is for your home. For a short trip, use stay.

  • Do you have any problem for VPN connection?

    Say it naturally

    Did you have any problems with the VPN?

    A finished trip needs did. Use any + plural (problems), and problems with something.

  • When are you finished your bachelor's degree?

    Say it naturally

    When did you finish your bachelor's degree?

    Completed past event: did + subject + base verb. Did already shows the past, so finish stays in its base form.

Pattern 2

Other questions

  • Why only you in this room now?

    Say it naturally

    Why are you the only person in this room now?

    This question needs are. Compare: You are alone → Why are you alone?

  • How many year people learn in school?

    Say it naturally

    How many years do people spend at school?

    How many + plural noun. For a general question in the present, use do.

  • What kind of animals was new for you?

    Say it naturally

    What kind of animals were new for you?

    Animals is plural, so use were.

  • I can you tell about my city?

    Say it naturally

    Can I tell you about my city?

    In a question, the helper verb comes first: Can I tell you…?

  • You need to pay for visit this place or no?

    Say it naturally

    Do you need to pay to visit it, or is it free?

    Pay to + verb. Start with do for a general question, and finish with or is it free, not or no.

Present habit
Do / does + person + base verb?Do you share reels with your friends? What does your friend usually share?
Past event
Did + person + base verb?Did you visit Moscow? Where did you stay? When did you finish university?
A situation
Am / is / are / was / were + person?Are you at home? Why are you alone? Were you in Russia for two weeks?
Duration
How long…?How long does a PhD take? How long did you stay in Moscow?

Your school question“How many years do people spend at school?” asks for a number of years. “How long do people spend at school?” asks about duration. Both work. After did, use finish, not finished.

Fix it

Small words, big difference

These small changes are easy to miss when you speak fast: -s on numbers, was or were, for, and verb partners.

  • In Russia we study nine year and go to college or 11 year and go to university.

    Say it naturally

    In Russia we study for nine years and then go to college, or for eleven years and then go to university.

    After a number, add -s: nine years. Use for with a length of time.

  • I finished in 2021 year, plus two year master's degree.

    Say it naturally

    I finished in 2021, plus a two-year master's degree.

    Say the year alone: in 2021. Before a noun, the number part stays singular: a two-year degree, but two years.

  • And you was in Russia around two weeks.

    Say it naturally

    And you were in Russia for around two weeks.

    You takes were. Use for before a length of time.

  • My wife want to visit it.

    Say it naturally

    My wife wants to visit it.

    With he, she or one person (my wife), add -s: she wants.

  • I can talking about my experience. We can discuss about history.

    Say it naturally

    I can talk about my experience. We can discuss history.

    After can, use a base verb. Talk about a topic, but discuss a topic without about.

  • You recommend visit this place?

    Say it naturally

    Do you recommend visiting this place?

    After recommend, use -ing: recommend visiting. Start with do for the question.

  • I was on second course in bachelor's degree.

    Say it naturally

    I was in the second year of my bachelor's degree.

    In Russian, курс means a year of study. In English, say the second year. A course is a subject or a programme.

Have you ever been…?

When you talked about places you know about but haven't visited, you used the present. For experience in your life, English uses have.

One time in the past

use the past simple, with or without a time

  • I went there once.
  • We visited in September.

Experience in your life

have + been / seen / heard

  • I've never been there.
  • Have you ever seen a sea lion?

  • I hear about it, but I don't visit.

    Say it naturally

    I've heard about it, but I've never been there.

    For experience in your life, use have + past participle. Never goes between have and been.

  • In past I visit, but I'm not sure it's ballet.

    Say it naturally

    I went once, but I'm not sure it was ballet.

    A finished event in the past: went. Don't use in past; say once, last year or two years ago.

  • I don't visit ballet dance.

    Say it naturally

    I've never been to a ballet.

    Say have never been to a place or an event. Say a ballet, not ballet dance.

Words · quiz

Words to learn and revisit

Some words you checked, some are useful phrases from the conversation. First the pairs that are easy to mix up.

storyvshistory

a temporary post that disappears after 24 hours, or a tale about events

history: the past of a country, city or person

In Russian, история can mean both. In English they are different words.

convenientvscomfortable

easy to use or suitable for your situation

comfortable: pleasant to sit or sleep in

In Russian, удобный can mean both. A group chat is convenient; a chair is comfortable.

feel guiltyvsblame

feel bad because you think you did something wrong

blame: say that someone else is responsible: blame someone for something

sold outvsoccupied

all the tickets are sold

occupied: a seat or a place that someone is using now

Check yourself

  1. My chair is very    . I can sit in it for hours.

  2. A group chat is     because I can message everyone at once.

  3. I felt     after wasting my whole morning on my phone.

  4. We couldn't buy tickets. The performance was    .

  5. Instagram     disappear after 24 hours.

Listen · repeat

Listen and repeat

Press ▶ to hear each word (in an Indian English voice if your device has one), say it twice, then read the note. These are listening reminders, not a list of mistakes.

  • queue KYOO

    One syllable, like the letter Q. The extra letters do not add syllables.

  • giraffe juh-RAAF

    Stress the second syllable. The first sound is j, as in job.

  • guilty GIL-tee

    Two syllables. Start with a hard g, as in go.

  • addictive uh-DIK-tiv

    Stress the middle syllable.

  • algorithm AL-guh-rith-um

    Stress the first syllable. The ending is -rith-um, not -rhythm.

  • ballet BAL-ay

    A common British pronunciation. The final t is silent. American English also commonly stresses the last syllable: bal-AY.

Speaking

Keep the conversation going

Speak first, then open the example. Record yourself if you can and listen for past verbs.

Interview linkInterviewers often say, “Tell me about a time when…” Answer in the past: what happened, what you did, and what the result was. It is the same skill as your Instagram story.

  1. 1

    Tell me about your experience in Moscow. Ask me two follow-up questions about mine.

  2. 2

    Introduce your home city to someone who has never visited. Describe one place and one part of its history.

  3. 3

    Tell a story about a time you learned something new quickly. Use three sentences, all in the past.

    Compare with an example answer

    Last year I had to learn something new quickly. I watched tutorials every evening and asked friends for help. After two weeks, I could do it by myself.

If you need a moment, say “Let me think” or “How can I put this?” If you lose the word, describe what it does or what it looks like.

For our next conversation, prepare a short story about Moscow or an introduction to your city. Practise once for the meaning, then again for past-tense verbs.
