Fix it

Your sentences, naturally

These are things you said in our two lessons. Read what you said, try to say it better out loud, then open the natural version.

Talking about time and effort

  • I scattered my energy into many different areas.

    Say it naturally

    I spread myself too thin across too many activities.

    A fixed expression for when your attention is divided between too many things.

  • I still not good at technical skills and English.

    Say it naturally

    I'm still not very good at technical skills or English.

    Don't forget the verb: I'm good at, I'm not good at. Use or after not.

  • I hope I can reduce my time on other activities.

    Say it naturally

    I hope I can spend less time on other activities.

    We spend time on an activity. Use less time, not reduce my time.

  • Devote most of my time to the skill learning about English and technical.

    Say it naturally

    I want to devote most of my time to improving my English and developing my technical skills.

    devote time to + noun or -ing. Technical describes a noun: technical skills.

  • Everyone's time is same.

    Say it naturally

    Everyone has the same twenty-four hours each day.

    Say the same, never just same. Everyone takes has, not have.

Small things to check

Each one is a small change, but together they make you sound much more natural.

  • If I want to make obsessive effort on English learning…

    Say it naturally

    If I want to put more effort into learning English…

    We put effort into something. Into + noun or -ing.

  • …then I should trade off other activity.

    Say it naturally

    …then I should give up some other activities.

    After other, many, some and different, use a plural noun. Say the final -s so people hear it.

  • It's a noun and an adjective in different situation.

    Say it naturally

    It's a noun or an adjective in different situations.

    Different + plural noun: different situations, different ways.

  • I can continue read.

    Say it naturally

    Shall I continue reading?

    continue + -ing (or continue to read). Use Shall I…? to offer.

  • So, why I choose this paragraph because it inspires me.

    Say it naturally

    I chose this paragraph because it inspired me.

    Give the answer, then because. The choice was in the past, so: chose.

  • If we pick one, we will lose another.

    Say it naturally

    If we pick one, we lose the other.

    When there are only two things, the second one is the other. Another means one more.

  • I think it's similar with long for, right?

    Say it naturally

    I think it's similar to long for, right?

    We always say similar to. (You said it correctly later in the lesson.)

04

Make the language yours

Answer aloud without reading from the article.

  1. Describe a time you spread yourself too thin.

    I often spread myself too thin. I make lots of plans and schedule too many activities, trying to fit in time for my family, my friends and myself.

    Useful pattern: spread myself too thin by trying to do too many things.
  2. Name one trade-off you may need to accept to reach a goal.

    When I have free time, I usually arrange activities with my friends or go out with them. Now I am choosing to prioritise English. The trade-off of spending more time learning English is that I have less time to spend with my friends.

    Another way: Spending more time learning English means having less time for my friends.
  3. Describe a genuine breakthrough in your work or learning.

    About five years ago, I took around fifty English classes with a teacher from the Philippines, usually three classes a week. After three or four months of focused study, I made a real breakthrough in my listening. I could understand my customers much better in meetings. Later, I stopped practising regularly, and my listening became rusty. Now I want to practise more than three times a week so I can improve my listening and spoken English again.

    Useful patterns: make a breakthrough in something; become rusty when you stop practising.
  4. Make one sentence with crave and another with long for.

    Long for: I long for a change to my room.

    Crave: I crave the chance to see my idol this year.

    Crave + noun: “the chance to see my idol.” In everyday conversation, you could also say, “I really want to see my idol this year.”
