Fix it

Two things to check when you speak

Correct each sentence out loud before you open it. These are from our conversation, tidied slightly from the recording.

Check 1

Past time → past verb

  • I also have a meeting with customers last Friday.

    Say it naturally

    I also had a meeting with customers last Friday.

    Last Friday is in the past, so the verb is too. Find the time phrase first, then check the verb.

Check 2

Number → plural noun

  • Some word… two sentence…

    Say it naturally

    some words · two sentences

    After two, three, many, some or several, add -s.

  • Another problems.

    Say it naturally

    another problem

    Another means one more, so the noun stays singular.

  • Leaders or other person, I think.

    Say it naturally

    Leaders or other people, I think.

    Some plurals change completely: person → people, child → children.

Later, one at a time

Don't try to fix all of these at once. Pick one per week.

  • I don't need to focus the very specific word.

    Say it naturally

    I don't need to focus on specific words.

    Learn the verb with its partner: focus on, listen to, ask for, explain something to someone.

  • Listen what I'm talking.

    Say it naturally

    Listen to what I'm saying.

    Listen to something. Say what; talk about something.

  • They have a little confused.

    Say it naturally

    They were a little confused.

    A person is confused. A thing is confusing. The feeling is confusion.

  • What's the means of recurring?

    Say it naturally

    What does recurring mean?

    What does ___ mean? is the standard question for any new word.

  • Please check whether have any mistake.

    Say it naturally

    Please check whether there are any mistakes.

    After whether, you still need a full sentence: subject and verb.

  • I don't know whether I have making mistakes.

    Say it naturally

    I don't know whether I'm making any mistakes.

    For something happening now, use am / is / are + -ing. Have making doesn't exist.

  • I usually use I or we start.

    Say it naturally

    I usually start with I or we.

    Start with and begin with go together. Put the verb first.

  • Expected they could understand, but no.

    Say it naturally

    I expected them to understand, but they didn't.

    expect someone to do something. Also remember the subject: I expected.

  • See you next the class.

    Say it naturally

    See you next lesson. / See you in our next lesson.

    No the and no preposition before next: next week, next Friday, next lesson.

  • When my team has a problem, I step into help.

    Say it naturally

    When my team has a problem, I step in to help.

    Step in = get involved to help. Step into = start a new role: She stepped into the manager role.

Words · quiz

The words you highlighted

Each word has a partner that people often mix it up with. Knowing the pair is what makes the meaning stick.

briefingvsdebriefing

a short meeting or message that gives people the information they need before something happens

debriefing: happens after, to review what happened

beyondvswithin

further than a point or limit. In a workflow, “approved and beyond” means that stage and every stage after it

within: inside the limit

recurringvsongoing

happening again and again, with breaks in between

ongoing: continuing without stopping

spillovervsbacklog

unfinished work from one sprint that moves into the next

backlog: all the work still waiting to be planned

Sentence builder

Saying no politely: bandwidth

At work, bandwidth means the time and attention you have available. Here is your idea from the lesson, in three pieces:

  1. Given that I already have a lot of work,the reason
  2. taking on a new tasktake on = accept responsibility for
  3. would be beyond my bandwidth.more than I can handle

ShorterI don't have the bandwidth to take on a new task right now.

OppositeThat's within our bandwidth. We can do it this week.

4

Compare

Everyday words, work words

Both are correct English. The work version is more precise, not more correct. Simple English is always fine in an email.

  • We have a problem.→We're facing a challenge.Challenge sounds like something to overcome. Problem and issue are still fine at work.
  • Tell everyone the important details.→Brief the team on the key points.Brief someone on something.
  • Tell me what's happening.→Keep me updated on progress.
  • We need to agree on what matters most.→We need to align on our priorities.Align on = reach the same understanding. Agree is perfectly natural too.
  • We can't take on more work right now.→We have limited capacity for additional work.
  • Please send it by Wednesday.→Please submit it by Wednesday.Submit = send for review or approval. For a normal message, send is right.
5

Patterns

Starting a sentence

You said many of your sentences start with I or We. That's fine. When you want variety, start with the purpose, the benefit or the situation.

Three ways to begin

  • The purpose of this meeting is to agree on the next steps.
  • The main benefit of planning ahead is that we can review requests earlier.
  • Given our current workload, we need to prioritise urgent requests.

so that or such as?

so that
gives the reason or goalReview the list early so that we can prepare.
such as
gives examplesReview urgent requests, such as system fixes.

  • Plan the next two sprints in advance, such as review early.

    Say it naturally

    Plan the next two sprints in advance so that we can review requests early.

    Here you are giving the reason, not an example, so use so that.

Pronunciation

Bold shows the stressed part. Press ▶ to hear it (in an Indian English voice if your device has one), say the word, then read the sentence.

  • scenarios sih-NAIR-ee-ohz

    Let's consider two possible scenarios. End with a clear /z/.

  • technology tek-NOL-uh-jee

    Technology can help us work faster. ch sounds like k.

  • modernizing MOD-er-ny-zing

    We are modernizing the way we work. The s/z is a /z/ sound.

  • adjective AJ-ik-tiv

    Recurring is an adjective. d + j join into one sound.

  • real REE-uhl

    This is a real opportunity. Glide from ee into l. One or two syllables are both fine.
