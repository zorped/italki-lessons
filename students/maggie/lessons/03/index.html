Precise words and natural patterns

Compare similar words, then practise the sentence patterns that will make your explanations clearer and more natural.

3.1

Words that are close—but not identical

Learn the difference through the example, not through a synonym alone.

merely · barely · hardly · rarely

High priority
merely

only or simply

It was merely a suggestion.
barely

only just; almost not

I barely had enough time.
hardly

almost not

I could hardly hear the speaker.
rarely

not often

I rarely work on Sundays.

Barely and hardly describe degree. Rarely describes frequency. Merely makes something seem less important or complicated.

fate · destiny · luck · chance

Useful next
fate

an outcome believed to be unavoidable

He accepted his fate.
destiny

a future or purpose someone is believed to be meant for

She felt it was her destiny to teach.
luck

good or bad fortune affecting a result

Good luck helped, but she also prepared well.
chance

random possibility—or an opportunity

Success should not be left to chance.

Fate and destiny can suggest that events are meant to happen. Luck and chance do not suggest a fixed future.

setback · obstacle · delay · failure

High priority
setback

a problem that temporarily slows progress

The setback delayed the project, but it did not end it.
obstacle

something that blocks the way

Cost was the main obstacle.
delay

a wait or a later-than-planned event

The shipment arrived after a short delay.
failure

a lack of success

One failed attempt is not a permanent failure.

A setback is temporary. A failure describes an unsuccessful result; it does not automatically mean all future attempts will fail.

area · field · realm

Useful next
area

a general subject or part of an activity

Listening is one area I want to improve.
field

a recognised subject or profession

Logistics is her field.
realm

a sphere of knowledge or activity; more formal

That question is outside my realm of expertise.

Area is the most flexible everyday word. Field often refers to work or study. Realm sounds more formal and usually needs extra context.

genuine · true · genuinely · truly

Useful next
genuine

real, authentic or sincere

Her interest was genuine.
true

factually correct

The story is true.
genuinely

sincerely or really

I was genuinely surprised.
truly

really; in fact

It was a truly remarkable result.

Genuine and true are adjectives. Genuinely and truly are adverbs, so they describe actions, descriptions or whole ideas.

long for · crave · want · be eager to

Extension
long for

want something deeply over time

I long for a change.
crave

feel a powerful desire for something

I crave some quiet time.
want

the neutral everyday choice

I want to improve my listening.
be eager to

strongly want to do something

I am eager to meet my idol.

Use long for + noun, but long to + verb. Crave normally takes a noun or an -ing form—not an infinitive with to.

3.2

Four grammar patterns to keep

Countable or uncountable?

many / few / fewer + plural countable noun

many tasks · a few classes · fewer activities

much / little / less + uncountable noun

much work · a little time · less English

Verb patterns

spend time + -ing: I spent time practising.

continue + -ing: I continued learning.

long for + noun: I long for a change.

be eager + to: I am eager to improve.

Tell a past story in the past

Five years ago, I enrolled in several English classes. After a few months, I realised I had made a breakthrough in my English listening.

Anchor the story with a past time, then keep the main events in the past.

Useful noun partnerships

make a breakthrough in listening

enrol in a course

a bachelor's degree

work on holidays

3.3

Turn the idea into natural English

Focus on the reusable pattern. These are polished versions of ideas from our conversation, not a test.

work too much tasks at the same time

work on too many tasks at the same time

I planned too much work and activities for today.

I planned too much work and too many activities for today.

I spent a few time taking care of my child.

I spent a little time taking care of my child.

It consumes much time for him to finish the work.

It takes him a lot of time to finish the work.

I didn't continue to learning.

I didn't continue learning.

I crave to see my idol this year.

I am eager to see my idol this year.

I have to trade off many hobbies.

The trade-off is having less time for my hobbies.

I always confuse this word with destiny.

I always mix up fate and destiny.

3.4

Use the distinctions aloud

01

Explain why merely cannot replace rarely in “I rarely work on Sundays.”

02

Describe a work setback. Was it an obstacle, a delay or a complete failure?

03

Make four short sentences using fate, destiny, luck and chance.

04

Describe one trade-off of improving English quickly. Use less once and fewer once.

05

Tell your breakthrough story again using enrolled, realised and had made.

06

Choose naturally: “I long for / crave / want / am eager to improve my listening.” Explain your choice.

Aim for accurate patterns first; speed will follow with repeated speaking.
