Fix it

Your sentences, naturally

These are lightly tidied reconstructions of your own speech. Try the correction aloud before opening the example.

  • Different people have different method to finish the task.

    Say it naturally

    Different people have different methods for completing the task.

    Different people can have several methods, so use the plural. This is the same plural-noun check from our earlier lessons.

  • We can summary some common words or phrase.

    Say it naturally

    We can summarise some common words and phrases.

    Summary is a noun; summarise (or summarize) is the verb. After can, use the base verb.

  • My family members think I'm a person that very kind and nice.

    Say it naturally

    My family members think I'm a person who is very kind and nice.

    The description needs a verb: who is kind. A shorter version is 'My family members think I'm very kind and nice.'

  • The view is same across the regions.

    Say it naturally

    The view is the same across the regions.

    Learn the same as a complete phrase. We also met this in 'Everyone has the same twenty-four hours.'

2

Words

Your workplace words

Learn each word with its sentence partners. Start with the group you are most likely to use this week.

People and working together
alongside
together with, or next to someone or somethingI work alongside colleagues from different teams.
frontline
directly involved in serving customers or doing the main operational workFrontline colleagues understand the problems customers face.
blend
a combination of different things; also a verb meaning combineThe role needs a blend of technical knowledge and communication skills.
encompass
include a range of things within a larger wholeMy role encompasses planning, reporting and customer communication.

Learn the complete patterns: work alongside someone; a blend of A and B; a role encompasses several responsibilities.

Improving the way we work
strengthen
make something stronger or more effectiveRegular updates strengthen communication between teams.
scale
expand an activity or system so it can handle more workWe need a process that can scale as the business grows.
scalable
able to expand to handle more work or reach more peopleA scalable training programme can reach teams in many locations.
framework
an organised structure of principles or steps that guides workThe framework helps us assess skills consistently.
initiative
a planned effort to improve something; also the ability to act without being toldThe training initiative will help new colleagues. She took the initiative to organise it.
initiation
the act or process of starting somethingDuring project initiation, we agree on the purpose and scope.
simplify
make something simpler or easier to understand or doThe new process will simplify decision-making.

A framework guides the work. An initiative is an organised effort to make a change. They are not interchangeable.

Skills and standards
consistent
following the same standard or behaving in a steady, reliable wayWe need consistent service across all locations.
rigor / rigour
careful, thorough attention to methods, accuracy and standardsThe review needs more rigor so that we can trust the results.
thoroughly
completely and carefully, with attention to all the detailsPlease check the report thoroughly before sending it.
applicable
relevant and able to be used in a particular situationThese guidelines are applicable to all teams.
competency / competencies
an ability to do something well; at work, competencies are specific skills or abilities needed for a roleCommunication is a key competency. The assessment covers several competencies.
expertise
specialist knowledge or skill gained through experienceThe team has expertise in warehouse operations.

Rigor is the American spelling; rigour is British. Competency is singular; competencies is plural.

3

Quiz · compare

Choose the right word

Use the sentence to decide which meaning and word form fit.

initiativevsinitiation

An initiative is a planned effort to improve something: “Our training initiative includes mentoring.”

Initiation is the start: “Project initiation includes agreeing on the scope.” Both words are nouns in these examples. Initiate is the verb.

consistentvscontinuous

Consistent means following the same standard: “Our teams use consistent criteria.”

Continuous means without a break. Visibility means being able to see or understand information. A consistent view is the same understanding across teams.

frameworkvsprocess

A framework provides an organising structure: “The framework identifies the skills to assess.”

A process is a sequence of steps: “The assessment process explains how to collect and review the results.”

scale→scalable

Scale can be a verb: “We can scale the training to reach more teams.” It can also be a noun: “We work on a global scale.”

Scalable is an adjective: “We need a scalable training programme.” Scaling expertise can mean sharing and applying specialist knowledge across more teams.

Pronunciation

Bold shows the stressed syllable. Listen, say the word, then use the complete sentence.

During the reading, we noticed missing plural endings and simplify being read as simple. Check the whole word, then its ending. The other words below are practice targets from today's vocabulary.

  • thoroughly THUR-uh-lee

    I checked the document thoroughly. Start with th as in think. Keep the final -ly.

  • competency KOM-puh-tuhn-see

    Communication is an important competency. Stress the first syllable. Competencies ends with a /z/ sound.

  • competencies KOM-puh-tuhn-seez

    The role requires several competencies. The plural ending is seez, not just see.

  • applicable AP-li-kuh-buhl

    This policy is applicable to our team. This common pronunciation stresses the first syllable. uh-PLIK-uh-buhl is also a standard variant.

  • rigor RIG-er

    We need more rigor in our review. The g is hard, as in go. British spelling: rigour.

  • initiative ih-NISH-uh-tiv

    She took the initiative to improve the process. Stress NISH. The middle sound is sh.

  • initiation ih-nish-ee-AY-shuhn

    The project is in the initiation stage. Compare the different endings: initiative ends in -tiv; initiation ends in -AY-shuhn.

  • strengthen STRENG-thuhn

    This will strengthen our working relationship. Keep the str at the beginning and the th in the middle.

  • simplify SIM-plih-fy

    We want to simplify the approval process. Read the whole ending. Simple ends in -puhl; simplify ends in -plih-fy.

  • areas AIR-ee-uhz

    The assessment covers several areas. Finish with /z/. We revisited this word during the reading.

  • perspectives per-SPEK-tivz

    The role combines technical and business perspectives. Keep the final /z/ so that the listener hears the plural.

Speaking

Say it out loud

Try using the new language in an explanation of your own. Open the example after you have spoken.

  1. 1

    Describe your responsibilities using encompass, alongside and frontline.

    Compare with an example answer

    My role encompasses planning and customer communication. I work alongside the operations team, and I share updates with frontline colleagues.

  2. 2

    Name two competencies you need for your role. Give an example of each.

    Compare with an example answer

    Communication and problem-solving are key competencies for my role. They help me understand a customer's concern and explain the next steps clearly.

  3. 3

    Propose an initiative to strengthen one part of your team's work.

    Compare with an example answer

    I would suggest a training initiative to strengthen communication. We could use a shared framework to make our updates more consistent.

  4. 4

    Explain what you would check before scaling a new process. Use thoroughly and applicable.

    Compare with an example answer

    Before scaling the process, we should test it thoroughly and check whether it is applicable to other locations.

  5. 5

    Explain your classroom example about different methods and consistent results. Check the plural endings.

    Compare with an example answer

    Different people may use different methods to complete a task, but their work should meet the same standard. That is what I mean by consistent results.

  6. 6

    Explain initiative and initiation using one project as an example.

    Compare with an example answer

    The training initiative is a programme to improve our skills. Its initiation is the stage when we set it up and agree on its goals.

6

Writing workshop

Your emails, made clearer

These revisions preserve your ideas. Names and system labels have been generalised. Open the sentence notes to see what changed.

1. Ask for missing information

Hi [name],

We are implementing multi-factor authentication, so each system account needs its own email address.

We found that many accounts with the specified prefix do not have an email address recorded in the system. Could you check these accounts and help us add the missing email addresses by the end of today?

Thanks for your help.

If this person only needs to check the accounts, say “Could you check these accounts today? We need to add the missing addresses by the end of the day.” This separates the responsibilities.

What changed?

  • require all the account have its own email address

    Say it naturally

    Each account needs its own email address.

    Each takes a singular noun and verb. Also possible: 'All accounts need their own email addresses.'

  • many accounts didn't have email addresses on the system

    Say it naturally

    Many accounts do not have an email address recorded in the system.

    We found describes the completed check. Do not have describes the problem that still exists.

  • could help check and we should add all the email address by today

    Say it naturally

    Could you check these accounts and help us add the missing email addresses by the end of today?

    Name who should act and give a clear deadline. Separate the project, problem and request into sentences.

2. Request a briefing

Hi [name],

The email below outlines several proposed changes to our processes and systems. Could you organise a short call to walk us through them?

This would help us understand the changes and assess their impact more accurately.

Thanks for your help.

If the changes are confirmed, use “upcoming changes” in place of “proposed changes.”

What changed?

  • There will multiple process and system change based on blow email

    Say it naturally

    There will be several changes to our processes and systems, as outlined in the email below.

    Will needs a base verb: will be. Several changes is plural. Below refers to the email's position; blow is a different word.

  • could help organize a call to have a brief

    Say it naturally

    Could you organise a short briefing call?

    Could you + base verb forms a complete request. A briefing is an information session. Brief can mean short or be a verb: brief us on the changes.

  • It will help us better understanding and evaluate with more accuracy

    Say it naturally

    This would help us understand the changes and assess their impact more accurately.

    After help us, use a base verb (or to + base verb). Keep understand and assess parallel, and name what you are evaluating.

3. Express the benefit in your own words

  • this will help better coordination among teams which will reduce TCO to chase approval and refinement every time

    Say it naturally

    This will help teams coordinate better and reduce the need for our operations team to keep following up on approvals and refinement. These repeated follow-ups have been a recurring challenge.

    Help teams coordinate uses help + team/person + base verb. Reduce the need for someone to do something describes the benefit. Keep following up expresses repeated effort.

Check the source tooThe leader's original contains an error: “a recurring challenges” should be a recurring challenge. You can learn from the organisation of ideas while checking the grammar yourself.

A structure you can reuse

  1. SituationWhat are we doing, and why?
  2. Issue or impactWhat needs attention?
  3. RequestWho should do what, and by when?

Rewrite one email without looking. Check who should act, verb forms, plural nouns and the deadline. Then compare it with the example.
